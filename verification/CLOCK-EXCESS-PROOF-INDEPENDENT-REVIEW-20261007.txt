1. Divergent-excess proof

The native endpoint is actual_raw_occupation_divergent_eventual_lower in
formal-raw-divergent-excess-oct3/RawOccupationDivergentExcess.lean. It assumes
Odd u and injectivity of the actual shortcut orbit and concludes the
eventual ratio bound for every real b strictly below

  1/clockDrift + actualFirstHitDensity u /
                  (wholeOrbitCorrection u * log(3/2)).

For R eventually greater than one, multiplication by log R converts this
to the draft's displayed A(R)>=b log R. Odd u supplies positivity in Lean;
the paper states it explicitly. Injectivity makes u a divergent root,
which is enough to use the paper's P_infinity from the component proof.
The product is finite and at least one. The native coefficient and the
paper's eta(u) are therefore exactly the same, with no delta factor.

The finite future set has distinct odd targets because Syracuse time
embeds strictly into shortcut time. For sources reaching u, a prior visit
to a future target would force a shortcut repetition and hence contradict
the root's injectivity. First-hit weights factor exactly, including the
target u itself. For sources not reaching u, V_F is zero. Thus the
pointwise residual is nonnegative before any averaging or good-event
restriction.

The good descending contribution ends strictly before the first landing
at or below M. Every selected future target is first visited at or after
that landing, since u<=M. Their target sets are disjoint. This proves
Z_(R,F)>=L_R on the good event while preserving nonnegativity elsewhere.
The extra term is averaged on the entire root basin, including all bad
sources. Restricting it to good sources would lose the advertised exact
coefficient; the draft correctly does not do so.

For fixed R and F, odd logarithmic normalization is 2/log X. The weight
w_u has ordinary odd harmonic mean D_u/2, so V_F contributes exactly
D_u sum_F w_N(u). The residual's normalized mean is A(R) minus that term.
The same finite common-source union bound as the raw theorem therefore
gives precisely the draft's residual lower bound, with failure factor
2(n+2) alpha e(M). No independence or event density limit is assumed.

The logarithmic prefix J_R=floor(a log R/log(3/2)) has J_R/log R converging
to a/log(3/2). The actual growth bound puts every j<J_R target below R
eventually, since a<1 and u is fixed. Each forward first-hit correction is
at most P_infinity(u), so the reciprocal weight is at least its inverse.
The root term therefore contributes a eta(u) in the lower coefficient.
The grid p=a contributes a/lambda. First the source limit is taken at
fixed finite R,F_R and fixed aligned ladders; then R tends to infinity
with the bottom barrier fixed; then M tends to infinity with a and its
chosen finite grid size n fixed; finally a tends to one. These are the
same quantifiers discharged in AsymptoticResidualWithPrefix.lean.
For clarity, an explicit sentence fixing n after a and before M would
be harmless, but the reference to the preceding grid proof is sufficient.

The strict excess uses only the native unit positivity of D_u. A divergent
positive orbit reaches an odd unit: halve to an odd state and, if that
state is divisible by three, take one Syracuse step. Its tail stays
injective. The final scope statement correctly leaves the matching upper
bound unproved and does not infer either divergent exclusion or cycle
exclusion.

2. Inverse law and finite source normalization

The native inverse index a>=0 counts the even steps after the odd step;
the paper's exponent a>=1 is that index plus one. Thus its branch
(2^a x-1)/3 and coefficient 3/2^a are correct. Every retained endpoint is
positive, odd, and prime to three. Terms at nonunit endpoints have zero
trace. Expanding harmonicity leaves 2^(-j)g(2^j x); these targets all lie
in the fixed basin of x, so the paper's boundary theorem sends the
remainder to zero. The row sums to one. Countable-state Markov extension
is represented in the native file by Kernel.trajMeasure.

All inverse ancestors of a nonperiodic root are nonperiodic. At those
states g(y)=delta yD_y, so delta cancels in every telescoping prefix.
The affine product identity converts the remaining coefficient into
w_n(y_K). The cylinder mass is exactly w_n(y_K)D_(y_K)/D_n.

The source limit has factor 2/(D_n log X), because the mean is over odd
sources. First-hit factorization on the endpoint basin supplies the same
cylinder mass. Distinct valid prefixes at fixed depth have disjoint
endpoint basins: a common source would force a single deterministic
reverse suffix, using nonperiodicity for uniqueness of the hit. The
finite-source total is bounded above by the normalized full root mass,
which tends to one. Pointwise limits have total mass one. Hence a finite
head carrying almost all limiting probability also forces the empirical
tail small, proving total variation on the countable prefix set. No
uniformity in growing K or uncontrolled exchange of infinite sums is
used. The draft's shorter squeeze is exactly the native one; separate
estimates for omitted shallow sources are unnecessary here.

3. Last visits, transfer, and probability-one reference clocks

For real r>=n the finite set defining T_n(r) is nonempty and includes n.
It can include nonunit endpoints, which only makes the bound larger.
Every retained visit has first-hit odd depth equal to its inverse index.
All visits at height at most r therefore occur within the fixed prefix
of depth T_n(r), so sigma_r is finite and measurable from that prefix.
The real cutoffs correspond exactly to floor cutoffs in the Lean files.

For any odd source reaching the endpoint of a retained prefix long
enough to contain T_n(r), the first passage at or below r occurs at
k_n(q)-sigma_r. An earlier low state has remaining depth at most T_n(r),
so would appear later in that reverse prefix, contradicting maximality.
This proves tau_r(q)+sigma_r=k_n(q), even for paths that reenter the
barrier many times. Sources need not have any prescribed remote height.

Subtracting at M and x_i removes the full remote source depth. The native
lower-segment clock has error bounded by C_alpha(log x_i)^(3/5); adding
the bottom last visit costs at most T_n(M), and shifting the main term
adds log M/lambda. These are exactly the three terms in the draft.

For any finite I, take a prefix at least max_(i<=I)T_n(x_i). A single
simultaneous good ladder controls every stated inequality. The existing
odd-relative failure allowance 2 alpha e(M), weighted by w_n<=1 and
normalized by D_n, becomes 2 alpha e(M)/D_n. This matches
actual_finite_ladder_clock_failure_probability with no missing factor
of two. Fixed-prefix total variation transfers the bound. The failure
events increase with I, so continuity of probability preserves the same
bound for their union. It is not an infinite union bound over stages.

Replacing a fixed base M>1 by M^(alpha^J) removes J initial grid terms.
The reference-limit event is unchanged exactly. For each fixed J its
bottom correction is finite and vanishes relative to log x_i as i grows.
The failure bound tends to zero as J increases. Therefore each fixed
reference grid has its limit almost surely. This is the native shifted
finite-error argument, not an unjustified claim that one fixed good
ladder itself has probability one.

4. From reference clocks to every inverse height

For each integer L>=1, beta=alpha^(1/L)>1. With b=n+1, the L bases
exp(b beta^l) interlace exactly into exp(b beta^j). They all exceed n.
Only countably many bases are used over all L and l, so their almost-sure
events may be intersected. Finite interlacing gives the last-visit limit
on each refined grid.

Its last visits tend to infinity, so for sufficiently large k the least
j with sigma_(z_j)>=k exists and is positive. Maximality implies
Y_k>z_(j-1). In the other direction, Y_k is an actual forward Syracuse
iterate of Y_(sigma_(z_j))<=z_j after sigma_(z_j)-k steps. Thus the
growth inequality in the draft has the correct direction; inverse paths
themselves are not assumed monotone in height.

Writing g=log(3/2), the lower rate bound is lambda/beta. The upper bound
is beta lambda+(beta-1)g=lambda+(beta-1)log2, because lambda+g=log2.
Taking all integer L and sending beta down to one gives the claimed
almost-sure log Y_k/k limit. The native endpoint
actual_inverse_height_clock_ae has exactly the same four root hypotheses.
The paper correctly explains that nonperiodic means the root itself has
no positive return; its forward orbit can later enter a cycle.

The final paragraph correctly withholds expectation convergence,
uniform control across roots, a moving-height occupation upper bound,
and all cycle exclusion. The statement is about the actual integer
inverse law, not an unconditioned affine random walk or a Haar law.
