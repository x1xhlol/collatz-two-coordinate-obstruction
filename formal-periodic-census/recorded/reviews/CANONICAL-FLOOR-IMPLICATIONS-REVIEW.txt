Canonical floor: independent corollary review and cycle/divergence consequences
3 October 2026. Source reading and analytic deductions; no manuscript edits.

I read all of CanonicalTracePolynomialFloor.lean, its successful local
compile log, BasinWeightedDensityComparison.lean, and
ActualGreenDensityLimit.lean. I did not author this corollary module.
No semantic issue was found. Its three printed declarations have only
propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound in their displayed axiom closures.
This is not a claim of a fresh complete kernel replay by this reviewer.

Write K = predecessorSeedMultiplier and choose P >= 1 from the existing
same-target weighted-density comparison. The new statements give, with

  a = 3/(128*K),    b = a/P,    c = delta*b,

  d_N >= a/N,    D_N >= b/N,    g(N) >= c > 0

at every positive unit N modulo three. Here d_N is actual basin density,
D_N is the actual first-hit weighted density, and

  g(N) = delta * gamma(N) * N * D_N.

The cycle factor gamma is one off-cycle and 1/(1-beta) on-cycle. The
existing positive-cycle ratio theorem gives 0 < beta < 1, so gamma >= 1.
The code first divides d_N <= P*D_N by positive P, then multiplies by N
and delta, and finally uses gamma >= 1. It covers periodic targets as well
as off-cycle targets. No off-cycle identity has been applied on a cycle.

These are the actual implications I can establish, and the limits of the
known routes after incorporating them.

1. Disjoint basins give a reciprocal packing budget

For a finite set A of positive unit targets whose basins are pairwise
disjoint, finite additivity of their established natural densities gives

  a * sum_(N in A) 1/N <= sum_(N in A) d_N <= 1.

Consequently every such countable family satisfies sum 1/N <= 1/a,
interpreting the series as the supremum of finite subsums. This uses no
countable additivity assumption for arbitrary natural-density sets.

For pairwise disjoint side branches selected along an injective forward
orbit, this yields a uniform reciprocal-height budget. A sibling of the
form s=64*n+21 contributes at least a/(64*n+21), so the scale of the
resulting constraint is sum 1/n < infinity. This does not force finitely
many branches: a divergent height sequence can have a convergent reciprocal
series. In fact the already accepted distinct-path packing bound is itself
consistent with, and implies, reciprocal summability on an injective path.
There is no inverse-logarithmic branch budget here.

At two off-cycle unit sibling targets n and s=64*n+21, the unweighted and
weighted floors combined only with d_n,D_n <= 1 give respectively

  d_s/d_n >= a/s,    D_s/D_n >= b/s.

They do not give a constant times 1/log n. The trace floor gives
g(s)/g(n) >= c/g(n), and the off-cycle bound g(n)<=delta*n again gives
only a reciprocal-height estimate. Any improvement needs information
relating the masses at the two distinct targets.

2. Cycle injections are quantitative but still fit the cycle slack

For a primitive positive shortcut cycle n_0,...,n_(k-1), every phase is a
unit. At a cycle edge, inverse harmonicity gives

  g(n_(i+1)) = c_i*g(n_i) + R_i,

where c_i is 1/2 at even sources and 3/2 at odd sources. All R_i are
nonnegative. At an odd phase n_i, the other predecessor is 3*n_i+1,
so R_i = g(3*n_i+1)/2 >= c/2. These predecessor vertices are outside
the cycle but remain units, so the new uniform floor applies.

Let beta=product c_i=3^r/2^k. The exact slack identity now implies

  (1-beta)*g(n_0)
    >= (c/2) * sum_(i:n_i odd) product_(j>i) c_j.

This is a lower bound on the necessary cycle trace. It is not a
contradiction: the same cycle has the scalar amplification
gamma=1/(1-beta). There is no established upper comparison forcing the
displayed right side above the available left side.

One further consequence is available. Put v(n)=g(n)/n. At an odd cycle
source the exact identity is

  v(Tn) = [3*n*v(n) + g(3*n+1)]/(3*n+1),

and at an even source v(Tn)>=v(n). Thus u(n)=v(n)-c satisfies
u(Tn)>=a_i*u(n), with a_i=3*n/(3*n+1) at odd sources and a_i=1 at
even sources. The product of these a_i over the cycle is beta<1,
by the exact integer-cycle correction identity. Iterating once around
the cycle gives u(n)>=beta*u(n), hence

  g(n)/n >= c at every phase of every positive cycle.

This stronger cycle lower bound follows from the new uniform floor, but
it still supplies the same direction of information. The previous
exclusion criterion needs a comparison of g(3*n+1) with g(n)/n at every
odd phase, with enough strictness to prevent the cycle sum from vanishing.
Knowing both quantities are at least c does not compare them. In
particular, replacing the unknown g(n)/n by its lower bound in the
subtracted term of the cycle-increment identity has the wrong direction.

3. Divergent forward components remain compatible with the floor

I read ComponentRatioLimit.lean and the existing divergent-component
assessment to check this route. Along an injective positive orbit, the
established forward limits are

  d_(T^j n) -> component mass,
  D_(T^j n) -> component weighted mass,
  g(T^j n)/(T^j n) -> delta * component weighted mass.

Unit-basin positivity makes the relevant component mass positive whenever
such a component exists. The new floors strengthen finite-target lower
bounds, but a positive limiting density and trace growing proportionally
to forward height already satisfy them. The fixed-basin boundary theorem
controls targets tending to infinity inside one inverse basin; it cannot
be substituted for the unresolved upper boundary on an entire forward
component.

Similarly, the high-minimum construction inside a hypothetical divergent
component chooses a unit predecessor r=r(M) whose basin avoids all values
at most M. The new estimate gives high-minimum mass at least a/r(M), which
is positive for each M. The chosen r grows with M, so this does not supply
a positive lower bound independent of M and does not contradict the
known vanishing high-minimum tail. No controlled small r(M) has been
provided by that construction.

4. Occupation and transport formulas retain their missing estimate

The floor does bound the previously explicit endpoint factor:
1/g(Y_k) <= 1/c for every continuing unit endpoint. This is useful upper
control, but the whole-basin recoding criterion asks for a positive lower
share of the occupation lying in a low-distortion event, with the actual
height cutoff Y_k<=X. An upper bound on every reward does not prevent
nearly all of the occupation from lying in the complementary event.

For a paired likelihood, a lower bound on each of g(Y_k) and g(Y'_k)
does not upper-bound their ratio, establish uniform integrability, or
control the accumulated recoding distortion. The finite-prefix formulas
and within-root change of measure remain valid; no new paired change of
measure follows from the absolute floor alone.

Conclusion of this review: the corollary module is valid and supplies a
uniform positive trace floor, including on cycles. I found the new cycle
ratio lower bound and the reciprocal antichain budget above. Neither
closes a known cycle or divergence route. The missing relative mass and
cycle-injection comparisons are still substantive. This assessment makes
no Collatz-solution or near-solution claim.
